# FTCC SkillsUSA Baseline 2026–2027: Computer Programming and Web Design & Development (Postsecondary)

> **Status:** first-pass research report, 30 October 2026. It checks the original ChatGPT corpus claim by claim. [`02-verified-baseline.md`](02-verified-baseline.md) supersedes it as the working reference.
>
> **Known correction:** the fees section below says NC state dues were not found and calls $14 a Tennessee figure. SkillsUSA NC's [join page](https://skillsusanc.org/join) lists $14 for students ($8 national + $6 state) and $26 for professionals ($20 + $6). The verified baseline has the correct figures.

Your background material is mostly sound on the big picture, but several details are wrong or come from a commercial vendor site, not from official rules: the attire, the professional development test timing, the scoring rubric weights and the schedule timings. The verified dates are: NC State Leadership and Skills Conference (SLSC), April 21–23, 2027, Greensboro; NLSC, June 21–25, 2027, Atlanta. The 2026–28 national Technical Standards for both contests are member-only on Pathful and are not public, so the advisor must download them before any rule in this report is treated as final.

## TL;DR

- The Web Design & Development (WDD) corpus is right about the core: Web Professionals Global (WPG) runs the event, teams have two members, divisions compete separately, the exam aligns to CWDSA-apprentice (70 questions, 60 minutes, and all 10 domain weights match WPG's blueprint), a live client sets the brief, there is a paper wireframing hour, a post-lunch "twist," interviews run during coding, and frameworks and AI are banned. It is wrong or unsupported on attire, PDT timing, exact phase timings, the 7-part rubric weights (no official source; treat as invented) and several details that trace only to ctelearning.com.
- Computer Programming (individual) is, under the latest public national standard (2024–26), a knowledge test plus 3–4 programming projects plus an interview. Competitors bring their own computer and software. Projects are scored on six criteria: completeness, correctness of output, validation of input, internal documentation, efficiency of code and quality of work. Each state may send one college/postsecondary entry. The 2026–28 text is not public.
- Critical dates: the national membership deadline is March 1, 2027. The NC South Central Regional Rally is at FTCC on February 19, 2027 (registration deadline February 3). NC SLSC is April 21–23, 2027. National online submissions are due June 3, 2027, and the national PDT is due June 17, 2027. NLSC runs June 21–25, 2027. FTCC has an active chapter. We found no past FTCC results in either contest.

## How to read this report

- **DOCUMENTED** means an official or primary source states the fact (skillsusa.org, skillsusanc.org, WPG/webprofessionals.org, webprocontests.org, a posted copy of a national Technical Standard, or faytechcc.edu).
- **CONFIRM** means the advisor must verify it with SkillsUSA NC, SkillsUSA national or the contest chair.
- **RECOMMENDATION** is our advice. It is not a rule.

## 1. Source quality warnings

- The corpus's primary source (citation 1) is "SkillsUSA Web Design: The 2026 National Prep Guide" on ctelearning.com. CTeLearning is a commercial curriculum vendor and a WPG partner. Its founder, Steve Waddell, has helped run the national event. That makes it informed, but it is still marketing content and not a rule document. Most corpus claims cite only this source.
- The corpus also cites unrelated or weak sources: a Reddit cabinetmaking thread, Idaho and Texas state documents, and a Scribd upload. None of these govern NC or national WDD rules.
- We could not fetch the "NC Web Design field guide" site (teacherbot.chatgpt.site). It is an AI-generated page, not an official source. We checked its three headline claims against official sources (see Table B).
- SkillsUSA states that Technical Standards "may not be posted or shared in any publicly accessible location" and are available to members through Pathful. Public copies on school sites (used below) are older editions. Treat them as baseline only.

## 3. Computer Programming baseline (individual, postsecondary)

**Status of the standard:** The 2026–28 national standard exists but is member-only on Pathful. We found no public copy. The facts below come from the 2024–26 national standard (public school-site copy). CONFIRM every item against the 2026–28 text.

### DOCUMENTED (2024–26 national standard)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Eligibility | Active SkillsUSA members in programs with computer programming as an occupational objective. Each state may send one high school and one college/postsecondary entry to nationals. |
| Scope | Uses competencies identified by CompTIA. |
| Knowledge test | Covers, "but not limited to," Java, C, C++, C#, Python, Ruby, JavaScript, PHP, Objective-C and SQL. The SkillsUSA PDT is also required. |
| Skill performance | A packet of three or four projects. Each project's specifications are written for one of the listed languages. The competitor must produce a successfully executable program from design notes and instructions. |
| Scoring criteria | Six criteria: completeness, correctness of output, validation of input, internal documentation, efficiency of code, quality of work. No weights are published. |
| Interview | Yes. It tests answers "typical of an entry-level position for a computer programmer." |
| Supplied by committee | Printer, programming instructions, timing and judges' procedures. |
| Supplied by competitor | Desktop and monitor or laptop. Software to write and run code in the language of choice. ONE copy of a coding reference manual (paper or digital). Pens or pencils, blank paper, one 6-foot surge protector. Online one-page resume. |
| Competency areas | CP 1.0 programming concepts; CP 2.0 Java; CP 3.0 C++ (including pointers and sequential files); CP 4.0 Visual Basic .NET (files, database controls, GUI); CP 5.0 SkillsUSA Framework. |
| Clothing | Class E: official SkillsUSA white polo, black dress slacks or skirt, black closed-toe dress shoes. |
| Resume | One-page PDF named "Last_First_Resume." Missing the online upload costs 10 points. |
| Devices | Phones, smartwatches and unapproved electronics are banned. Scores may be removed for misuse. |

### Recent national changes relevant to both contests (DOCUMENTED, NLSC 2027)

- The national PDT is online and must be finished before NLSC: deadline June 17, 2027, 5 p.m. ET. Credentials arrive by May 18, 2027.
- NLSC Wi-Fi is mainly 5 GHz. Competitors must pre-install fully licensed software. Unless the committee authorizes it, "internet access is not permitted during the competition." Software should run offline, and competitors need admin rights on their machines.
- Any competition-specific online knowledge test will be announced as a competition update by May 18.
- No Computer Programming update had been posted as of the October 22, 2026 page revision.

### NOT VERIFIABLE / CONFIRM

- The 2026–28 language list, any AI rule, any change to the interview, and any time limits.
- Whether the national knowledge test is given online before the conference.
- Public national recaps or sample problems. We found none from SkillsUSA. One state CS association lists 2026 high school national medalists only.

### RECOMMENDATIONS

- Pick ONE primary language now (Java, C# or Python fit FTCC programs best). Train all practice under contest rules: a single reference manual and no Internet.
- Build a laptop that is offline-capable, admin-enabled and runs from battery or a surge protector. Test it away from campus. NC warns that school IT locks have blocked students before.
- Drill the six scoring criteria on every practice problem. Make input validation and comments a habit.
- Run mock interviews on entry-level programmer questions.

## 4. Web Design & Development baseline (two-person team, postsecondary)

### DOCUMENTED

- **Format:** A one-hour online written exam, a WPG technical orientation on the cloud IDE, and a one-day practical. At NLSC 2026, the exam session ran noon–2 p.m. on Tuesday, secondary teams competed Wednesday and postsecondary teams competed Thursday, 8 a.m.–3 p.m.
- **Practical flow:** a live client interview, one hour of paper wireframes, coding in the WPG online IDE, a post-lunch twist, and team interviews during coding. The 2026 interviews covered design decisions, wireframe process, and why the agency should be chosen.
- **Rules:** No templates. All work must be created during the contest. Copyright laws apply, and violations mean disqualification. No front-end frameworks. No AI generation assistance. At state contests run with WPG's package, WPG's 2025 State Competitions Recap says "Teams are not allowed to use frameworks of any sort (or help from AI), as doing so results in disqualification."
- **Equipment (2023-24 standard):** The committee supplies table space and power. The team brings two surge protectors, a sketch pad, computers with 5 GHz/Wi-Fi 6, and wireframing and documentation tools.
- **Exam blueprint:** CWDSA-apprentice domains and weights (Table A, rows 4–5).
- **Field size:** WPG's 2025 recap says "We had roughly a dozen post-secondary teams" and that "Roughly two dozen states were represented" in the secondary division. There were 26 secondary teams in 2026. The 2025 postsecondary gold went to Pinellas Technical College-Clearwater (Fla.).
- **State events:** WPG supplies many states with a "competition in a box" (exam, project, rubric and IDE). WPG's 2026 State Competitions Recap lists the states it ran spring 2026 contests for: Alabama, Arizona, Colorado, Florida, Hawaii, Illinois, Indiana, Louisiana, Mississippi, Ohio and Tennessee. North Carolina is not on that list. CONFIRM how NC runs its contest.

### RECOMMENDATIONS

- Assign fixed roles: one designer/UX lead and one front-end developer. Cross-train both on HTML/CSS for the exam, since exam domains are 50% HTML and CSS.
- Use the official CWDSA-apprentice blueprint as the written-test syllabus. Use MDN for HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Use the W3C Nu HTML Checker and the W3C CSS Validator for code. Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the accessibility target.
- Practice in a browser-only editor with no frameworks, no AI and no templates. Write CSS with custom properties so the twist is cheap to absorb.
- Run full 7-hour simulations: client interview, 60 minutes on paper, coding, a twist after lunch, and an interview during coding.
- Do not use the corpus rubric percentages for scoring practice. Get the current sample rubric from SkillsUSA (member SharePoint) or WPG through SkillsUSA.

### Fees (DOCUMENTED, 2026 pattern; CONFIRM 2027)

- Regional rally: $20 per person (contestant and advisor).
- NC state registration: $60 per member who competed at a regional rally; $85 for those who did not.
- Late additions: $25 each. Changes after the drop deadline: $25, or $50 onsite. Card payments carry a 4% fee. Your school's balance must be paid before your students can compete.
- National dues: $8 per student (skillsusa-register.org) and $20 per professional member, per South Dakota SkillsUSA ("National dues for professional members are $20 plus state dues"). The $14 figure seen elsewhere is a student total in Tennessee, not a professional rate. NC state dues: not found. CONFIRM. *(Corrected later: see the status note at the top.)*

### NC procedures (DOCUMENTED, 2026 state conference)

- Official contest attire is required at the contest, opening and awards ceremonies.
- Resumes are handed in on paper at the contest. Penalty points apply if one is missing.
- No online PD test was given at state in 2026.
- Phones and unapproved electronics are banned.
- Students who bring computers must test software access off campus beforehand.

### Qualification path (DOCUMENTED)

- First place at NC SLSC in a national contest qualifies the student or team for NLSC.

- Nationally, each state may send one postsecondary Computer Programming entry.
