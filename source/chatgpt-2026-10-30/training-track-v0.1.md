# Web Development | SkillsUSA Training Track

Working coach plan v0.1 | October 30, 2026

## Scope and caution

The North Carolina contest list calls this event “Web Design”; the national SkillsUSA name is “Web Design and Development.” The recovered October 9 site and research document provide useful practice ideas, but their detailed timing, software bans, point values and exam specifications must not be treated as current 2026–28 rules without member/organizer confirmation.

## Student outcomes

- Build and explain a functional, accessible, responsive website from a brief.
- Interpret user needs, identify constraints, plan navigation and content, and defend design choices.
- Author valid semantic HTML; use CSS layout and breakpoints deliberately; implement simple JavaScript behavior and keyboard-friendly controls.
- Check responsive behavior, labels, color contrast, images, file references and browser console errors.
- Respond to a mid-project change without losing a working baseline.
- Communicate responsibilities and the handoff between design and implementation. Confirm official team size before assigning permanent roles.

## Competency map

- HTML: structure, navigation, links, forms, labels, headings, metadata.
- CSS: cascade, specificity, box model, Grid/Flexbox, responsive sizing and state styles.
- JavaScript: events, DOM selection, input checks, predictable user feedback.
- Design: visual hierarchy, content priority, wireframes and user tasks.
- Accessibility: semantic markup, meaningful text alternatives, keyboard navigation, visible focus, readable contrast.
- Verification: browser devtools, basic validation, path checks, smoke tests, documented known issues.
- Professional process: notes from the brief, time budgeting, partner handoffs and explanation to a reviewer.

## Baseline practice | Community Resource Desk

Scenario: A community resource desk needs a small site that helps residents find services. The client requests a home page, a short directory of three services, clearly marked hours, and a contact method. All names and information are fictional.

Timebox: 75 minutes build + 15 minutes test and explanation (local coaching exercise, NOT official competition timing).

Minimum deliverable: a working static web page with clear content hierarchy, mobile-friendly navigation, three service cards, and an accessible contact link or form stub.

Stretch: add a client-side category filter with plain JavaScript.

Change request (optional 20 minutes): the desk now needs an urgent weekend-hours notice that appears prominently without hiding the original content.

Constraints for baseline simulation: no AI code generation; no borrowed full-page templates; use local source files; do not assume these are official contest restrictions.

## Instructor practice rubric (100 points, not official)

- Functional requirements: 25
- Responsive presentation and readable visual hierarchy: 20
- Semantic markup and keyboard accessibility: 20
- Planning and client interpretation: 15
- Testing and defect repair: 10
- Clear explanation and team handoff: 10

Mark untested or externally sourced decisions in the coach log.

## Three-session coaching cycle

- A. Baseline build: observe planning, code ownership, defects, and completion.
- B. Repair clinic: each student selects two test failures or design faults and fixes them without a full rewrite.
- C. Pivot drill: deliver a new brief or tight change request; ask the team to preserve their tested baseline and explain tradeoffs.

## Corrections and additions, 30 October 2026 (Claude)

Claude checked this document against the FTCC SkillsUSA 2026–2027 Baseline report of 30 October 2026. The original text above is unchanged. Each line below has a status label. RESOLVED means a public official source answers an open item above. CORRECTED means a statement above conflicts with a documented fact. ADDED means the item is missing above. Items from the 2024–26 national standard must be checked against the 2026–28 standard in Pathful.

**CORRECTED (practice environment):** The baseline constraint "use local source files" does not match the national contest. At nationals, all coding happens in a server-based IDE that WPG supplies. Practice in a browser-based editor.

**RESOLVED (team size):** The national standard sets a team of two members. Assign fixed roles after NC confirms the size. Suggested split: one design and UX lead, one front-end developer. Cross-train both for the written test.

**RESOLVED (rules):** The WPG 2026 national recap states that templates, front-end frameworks (for example Bootstrap, Tailwind or React) and AI generation assistance are not permitted. Keep these constraints in every simulation, not only the baseline. Confirm the NC state rules.

**ADDED (written test):** The national exam aligns to the WPG CWDSA-apprentice certification: 70 questions in 60 minutes. HTML and CSS are 25 percent each. Use the official CWDSA-apprentice blueprint as the study syllabus. Confirm that the contest exam uses the same 70-question format.

**ADDED (contest flow to simulate):** A live client interview, one hour of paper wireframes, coding, a scope change after lunch, and team interviews during coding. The 2026 postsecondary day ran from 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. Add the paper hour and the interview during coding to the pivot drill.

**ADDED (rubric note):** The WPG 2026 public judging areas were UX/UI layout and responsiveness, semantic and accessible code, and teamwork and agency professionalism. The practice rubric above can map to these three areas. Get the current official rubric from SkillsUSA before you use any weights.

**RESOLVED (attire):** Class E business casual. Wear the official white SkillsUSA polo, black dress slacks or a black dress skirt (knee length minimum), and black closed-toe dress shoes. Socks are not required. If worn, they must be black dress socks. In NC, cover any school or business logo.

**ADDED (resume):** Each competitor needs a one-page PDF resume. A missing national upload costs 10 points. The national upload deadline is 3 June 2027, 5 p.m. ET. At NC state, competitors hand in a paper copy to the contest chair.

**ADDED (gate between planning and execution):** The planning gate is open when the advisor has two things. First, the 2026–28 Technical Standards for both contests, downloaded from Pathful. Second, written answers from SkillsUSA NC about entry limits, venue, how NC runs Web Design, and Internet and AI rules for Computer Programming. Before the gate, do only work that no rule can change: fundamentals drills, the two baseline exercises, written-test study, resumes, uniforms and membership. After the gate, lock the competition language, build the contest laptop, set the practice rubrics and schedule full mock contests.
